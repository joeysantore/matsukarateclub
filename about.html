How we train

Our base is Shotokan, but we treat it as a starting point. We practice striking, throwing, joint locks, throws, and other close-range work that modern karate has mostly stripped out.

We follow what holds up, wherever it comes from. Our training has been shaped by everyone from Mikio Yahara to Iain Abernethy to Rick Hotton. No single organization owns our thinking.

We’re not focused on competition, there are no mandatory honorifics, and rank is real and earned but doesn’t run the room.

Classes

Training is adults only, and classes are small on purpose. That way, everyone can get real attention – nobody disappears in the back of class.

There’s no standard class. We might spend all session on a single technique. Or we might practice only kata, partner work, or pad work. What we do depends on what the room needs.

The name

Matsu is Japanese for pine, and it’s a nod to a few things. Shotokan karate takes its name from shoto – "pine waves" – the pen name of Gichin Funakoshi, who brought karate to mainland Japan. Pine trees grow abundantly on the Okinawan coast, where the art was born. And a coastal pine is a particular kind of tree – bent by the wind, but rooted in place.

That’s the club, more or less. Shaped by good ideas from wherever they come, and not going anywhere.
