How we train

Our base is Shotokan, but we treat it as a starting point. We train kata for what they actually contain – the throws, locks, and close-range work that competition-era karate left behind – and we test the application against real resistance.

We follow what holds up, wherever it comes from. Our training has been shaped by everyone from Mikio Yahara to Iain Abernethy to Rick Hotton. No single organization owns our thinking.

We don’t compete, and keep minimal ceremony. Rank is real and earned, but it doesn’t run the room.

Classes

Training is adults only, and classes are small on purpose. That way, everyone can get real attention – nobody is going through the motions in the back of a crowd.

There’s no standard class. We might spend all session on a single technique. Or we might do only kata, or only partner work, or only impact drills on pads. Some days it’s more relaxed, some days it's more intense. What we do depends on what the room needs.

You don’t need experience to start – just an open mind.

The name

One last thing, since you made it this far.

Matsu is Japanese for pine, and it’s a nod to a few things. Shotokan karate takes its name from shoto – "pine waves", the pen name of the man who brought karate to mainland Japan. Many pine trees grow along the Okinawan coast, where the art was born. And a coastal pine is a particular kind of tree – shaped by the wind, but too rooted to come up.

That’s the club, more or less. Bent by good ideas from wherever they come, and not going anywhere.
